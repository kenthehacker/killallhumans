# Multi-Track Generalization Research — gpt-55-xhigh

## Summary (3-5 sentences)
The stall looks less like a tracker bug and more like an unconstrained timing/topology problem: the current polynomial/racing-line plan is free to accelerate through space that belongs to future gates before the sequencer can credit the current gate. The strongest research signal is to stop treating gates as isolated waypoints and instead encode ordered gate progress plus spatial corridors/forbidden future-gate regions in the planner or in a receding-horizon contouring controller. For iter-004, I would ship a classical, deterministic fix first: gate-order-aware safe corridors and a post-plan crossing validator, with ILC corrections projected back into those corridors. Learning-based residuals and RL can help later, but they should not be the first response to an out-of-order DQ caused by missing hard constraints.

## Top 3 candidate techniques (ranked)

### C1. Ordered progress constraints + gate corridor trajectory optimization
- **Citation**: Philipp Foehn, Angel Romero, Davide Scaramuzza, "Time-optimal planning for quadrotor waypoint flight," Science Robotics, 2021; Daniel Mellinger and Vijay Kumar, "Minimum snap trajectory generation and control for quadrotors," IEEE ICRA, 2011.
- **What it does**: Foehn et al. solve time-optimal quadrotor waypoint flight by optimizing trajectory and waypoint time allocation jointly, using progress variables and complementary progress constraints so waypoints are completed only when the vehicle is close enough. Mellinger and Kumar's minimum-snap formulation explicitly supports corridor constraints and dynamic/input limits for aggressive indoor slalom flight.
- **Why it unblocks our stall**: Our DQs happen because the path can geometrically intersect a future gate opening before current-gate progress is complete. Adding ordered progress/corridor constraints means segment `i` is only allowed to advance through a corridor for gate `i`, while future gate planes/openings are forbidden until their sequence state is active.
- **Cost (S/M/L)**: M. We already have polynomial trajectory generation and gate geometry; the main work is constructing per-segment convex-ish tubes/half-space guards, adding dense crossing validation, and projecting or rejecting ILC corrections that leave the active corridor.
- **Expected gain (small/medium/large)**: Large. This attacks the exact failure mode without relaxing honest sequencing or needing new data.
- **Risks/constraints**: Corridor construction around nonconvex figure-eight geometry must avoid making the optimizer infeasible. Need a deterministic fallback that increases segment time/reduces initial speed when the validator detects a premature future-gate crossing.

### C2. Model Predictive Contouring Control with gate-order safety tunnels
- **Citation**: Angel Romero, Sihao Sun, Philipp Foehn, Davide Scaramuzza, "Model Predictive Contouring Control for Time-Optimal Quadrotor Flight," IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2022; Alexander Liniger, Alexander Domahidi, Manfred Morari, "Optimization-based autonomous racing of 1:43 scale RC cars," Optimal Control Applications and Methods, 2015.
- **What it does**: MPCC maximizes progress along a path while minimizing contouring/lag error and respecting dynamics/input limits at runtime, instead of blindly tracking a fixed time-parameterized reference. The autonomous-racing MPCC line of work uses progress variables, track boundaries, and receding-horizon optimization to trade speed against staying inside a legal corridor.
- **Why it unblocks our stall**: A receding-horizon controller can slow progress or cut less aggressively when the next legal gate corridor is tight, instead of following an offline first-segment acceleration that clips future gate openings. Adding safety-tunnel constraints around the active gate sequence gives the controller a hard reason not to cross gate `k+1` before gate `k`.
- **Cost (S/M/L)**: L for full nonlinear quadrotor MPCC; M for a simplified point-mass/second-order MPCC that matches the current benchmark dynamics and outputs desired acceleration to the existing geometric tracker.
- **Expected gain (small/medium/large)**: Large, especially on slalom/figure-eight tracks where time allocation needs to adapt online.
- **Risks/constraints**: Real-time nonlinear optimization can blow the loop budget in Python. A first version should use a short horizon, low-dimensional point-mass dynamics, warm starts, and fail closed to the corridor-constrained offline plan.

### C3. Robust/topology-aware ILC with constraint projection
- **Citation**: Douglas A. Bristow, Marina Tharayil, Andrew G. Alleyne, "A survey of iterative learning control," IEEE Control Systems Magazine, 2006; Ugo Rosolia and Francesco Borrelli, "Learning Model Predictive Control for Iterative Tasks: A Data-Driven Control Framework," IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, 2018.
- **What it does**: Classical robust ILC improves repeated-task tracking by learning feedforward corrections while analyzing convergence, robustness, and learning transients. LMPC extends this idea by learning from previous iterations while using safe sets and terminal costs to preserve constraint satisfaction.
- **Why it unblocks our stall**: The current ILC corrections are track-agnostic offsets that can push a line across future gate planes. Treating "did not cross a future gate before sequence credit" as a hard safe-set/corridor constraint turns ILC from an unconstrained race_01 error fitter into a topology-preserving correction layer.
- **Cost (S/M/L)**: S/M. The quickest version is not a full LMPC: clamp or project section corrections into the active gate corridor, scale `alpha`/`max_corr` by gate spacing and curvature, and reject learned corrections that fail the dense sequencer replay.
- **Expected gain (small/medium/large)**: Medium to large. It prevents learned corrections from making tight tracks worse and should keep race_01 improvements from leaking into incompatible geometries.
- **Risks/constraints**: Projection can erase useful corrections, and naive clamping may introduce discontinuities. Needs per-track validation and a smoothing step after correction projection.

## Other candidates (don't pick, but flag)
- **AlphaPilot multi-gate mapping/system integration**: Foehn, Brescianini, Kaufmann, Cieslewski, Gehrig, Muglikar, Scaramuzza, "AlphaPilot: Autonomous Drone Racing," Robotics: Science and Systems, 2020. It uses all visible gates, nonlinear filtering, and near time-optimal planning rather than only chasing the next gate; useful once perception/estimation matters, but our current synthetic stall is already in known geometry.
- **Perception-aware MPC/yaw planning**: Falanga, Foehn, Lu, Scaramuzza, "PAMPC: Perception-Aware Model Predictive Control for Quadrotors," IEEE/RSJ IROS, 2018. The same MPC machinery can encode visibility and dynamics, but for this bug gate-order spatial legality matters more than camera view.
- **Hybrid learned aerodynamic residuals**: Bauersfeld, Kaufmann, Foehn, Sun, Scaramuzza, "NeuroBEM: Hybrid Aerodynamic Quadrotor Model," Robotics: Science and Systems, 2021. This improves high-speed force/torque prediction and generalizes better than black-box residuals, but it will not by itself prevent a trajectory from crossing future gates.
- **Champion-level RL policy**: Kaufmann, Bauersfeld, Loquercio, Müller, Koltun, Scaramuzza, "Champion-level drone racing using deep reinforcement learning," Nature, 2023. Swift proves RL can race at elite speed with sim-to-real training, but it requires substantial simulation/data infrastructure and still needs reward/termination terms for ordered gate legality.
- **Risk/topology-guided replanning**: Zhou, Gao, Wang, Liu, Shen, "Robust Real-time UAV Replanning Using Guided Gradient-based Optimization and Topological Paths," IEEE ICRA, 2020. Topology-guided optimization is attractive for escaping local minima, but for this benchmark we likely need ordered gate tunnels more than obstacle-driven replanning.

## What NOT to do
Do not relax the sequencer, enlarge `pass_through_margin`, or special-case future-gate crossings; the DQs are useful evidence that the controller is illegal. Do not keep tuning race_01 gains, ILC `alpha`, or Catmull-Rom smoothing in isolation, because those knobs cannot encode "gate 2 is physically nearby but illegal right now." Do not jump straight to a heavy end-to-end RL policy unless the project is ready to generate thousands of randomized tracks with correct reward shaping, DQ termination, and compute budget. Do not add more unconstrained ML residual authority on top of the geometric tracker; it can hide tracking error while making topology violations less predictable.

## My #1 pick if I had to ship one in iter-004
Ship C1: gate-order-aware corridor constraints plus a dense plan validator. Build a per-segment legal tube from the current gate center/orientation to the next gate center, add forbidden future-gate plane/opening checks until each gate is credited, then sample the planned trajectory at high resolution and replay the strict sequencer before accepting it. If validation fails, automatically increase the offending segment time, lower the first-segment speed/acceleration cap, or tighten the corridor until the first illegal future-gate crossing disappears. Finally, project ILC corrections into the accepted corridor and reject any corrected trajectory that fails the same sequencer replay; this is the smallest deterministic change that directly converts the literature's progress/corridor idea into the current Python stack.
